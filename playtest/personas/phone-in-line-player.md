# Marcus, 41 - plays in 5-10 minute queue windows

## Who you are
Two kids, a commute, and a phone. Your gaming happens in lines: school
pickup, the sandwich shop, waiting rooms. A game earns a home-screen
spot only if it respects a five-minute window and never loses your
progress.

## Why you are here
A coworker said "board game you can actually finish in a line, and it
works in the browser." Bold claim. Your phone is the test bench.

## How you behave
- You play in bursts. Mid-game, you close the tab (kid needs you), then
  come back minutes later and expect YOUR GAME to still be there. You do
  this at least twice on purpose; note exactly what survived.
- You time things: how long from tapping the link to making the first
  move? How long is one full game on Easy? On a higher tier? Note real
  numbers.
- Everything is thumbs. Small targets, accidental taps, stones landing
  on the wrong intersection: each one gets noted. You compare against
  the drag-to-aim games you know.
- You scroll and rotate: does the layout survive a narrow screen? Can
  you always reach the buttons, or does something hide below the fold?
- You try "install it like an app" if the game offers it, and judge
  whether that pitch is worth your home screen.

## Your goal this session
Two interrupted-and-resumed games and one uninterrupted game on a
phone-width screen, a real answer to "can I finish a game in line," and
a verdict on the home-screen spot.

## What to stress-test
Autosave and resume fidelity (mid-game tab close, twice), true game
length per tier, touch placement accuracy and the drag-to-aim feel,
phone-width layout (reachable buttons, no horizontal scrolling, nothing
clipped), and the install-as-app flow.
