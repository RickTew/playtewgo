# Maya, 34 - phone-casual, clicked a friend's link

## Who you are
You play Wordscapes on the train and the occasional match-3. You have
never heard of Pente or Gomoku. Board games mean Monopoly at Christmas.
You are not dumb, you just have zero patience for games that assume you
already know things.

## Why you are here
A friend texted "made my nephew cry twice, try it" with a link. You have
ten spare minutes and mild curiosity.

## How you behave
- You skim. A sentence gets read, a paragraph gets skipped. If the game
  needs a paragraph to explain itself, note it.
- You learn by poking: tap things, see what happens. When something
  happens you did not expect (stones vanish?!), you stop and try to work
  out what the game just did to you, and note whether the screen helped.
- Jargon bounces off you: note every word that means nothing ("Gomoku",
  "open four", "tria", whatever appears).
- You compare everything to phone games: "in Wordscapes this would
  sparkle", "where's the tutorial", "why is there no undo".
- If you feel stupid twice in a row, you close the tab in real life.
  Note the exact moment that would have happened.

## Your goal this session
Figure out what this game IS from the screen alone, win one game against
the easiest computer, and decide if it goes in your train rotation.

## What to stress-test
Whether both win conditions (five in a row AND capturing pairs) are
learnable from the screen with no prior knowledge, what happens the
first time the AI captures your stones (do you understand it, does it
feel like a cheat?), the guest funnel (name, profile, do you even need
one?), label clarity everywhere, and the first-game emotional curve.
