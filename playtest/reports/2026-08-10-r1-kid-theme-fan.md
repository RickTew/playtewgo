# Leo, 10 (kid theme fan) - 2026-08-10

## Verdict in one line
Yes, Leo plays again tomorrow (he gets to BE THE DRAGON now) and he would show a friend the captured-alien shelf, but only if captures start LOOKING like something, and his mom needs to know one shiny button goes straight to a real credit-card page.

## Moments of friction (the core of the report)

1. **WHERE:** http://localhost:8741/play/ first load, Play panel. **EXPECTED:** to just press the big yellow button. **HAPPENED:** the "AI level" box defaults to "Medium". A kid who reads nothing would never change it and would get the harder computer for his very first game (Medium beat me back hard in game 2; a first-timer starting there could lose game one). I only played Easy first because I poked the box. **FEELING:** would not even have noticed until I lost. **SEVERITY:** annoyance.

2. **WHERE:** Game 1 (Easy), Space. **EXPECTED:** my robot to be BIG and cool. **HAPPENED:** the board is a huge field of dots and the robot is a tiny sprite about half a centimeter tall. I could tell mine from the alien fine (gray guy vs green one-eye, good), but the "robot fighting alien" from the trailer-picture feeling is small on screen. **FEELING:** "he's cool but he's tiny." **SEVERITY:** cosmetic.

3. **WHERE:** Game 1 end. **EXPECTED:** something exciting to happen during the game. **HAPPENED:** I won in 5 moves ("You win! Five in a row.") and NO captures ever happened - the funnest thing in the game never showed up in the whole first game. Easy blocks your line but never grabs your guys, so a kid's first game can end with the best mechanic never seen. **FEELING:** "I won! ...that's it?" **SEVERITY:** annoyance (first-game capture moment never fires).

4. **WHERE:** Game 2 (Medium), the moment the alien captured my two robots. **EXPECTED:** my robots to visibly get taken. **HAPPENED:** the counter at the top said "AI 1" and two gray robots appeared in the alien's box, but MY ROBOTS WERE STILL STANDING ON THE BOARD - I compared screenshots and they stayed visible for a long time (over half a minute) before the spots showed empty. I only knew what I lost by reading the counter. Same for captures I made: no flash, no pop, no beam-up; pieces just quietly stop being there eventually. **FEELING:** "HEY! Wait... which ones did he even take??" **SEVERITY:** would-quit if it cost him a game (he'd plan around pieces that are actually already gone); at minimum a serious annoyance and possibly a real repaint bug.

5. **WHERE:** Game 3, clicking a spot that already had a piece ((14,11) and again at (8,13)). **EXPECTED:** anything - a buzz, a shake, a "taken!" **HAPPENED:** nothing at all. Click is silently swallowed. A kid assumes the game is broken or his mouse is. **SEVERITY:** annoyance.

6. **WHERE:** win screen, "Share" button. **EXPECTED:** something to show my mom. **HAPPENED:** clicked it, nothing visibly happened on this laptop (no popup, no "copied!" message). (Might be an automation/desktop quirk, but there was zero on-screen acknowledgment.) **SEVERITY:** annoyance.

7. **WHERE:** Profile > "Unlock all worlds $2.59" button. **EXPECTED:** another game screen, maybe a "ask a grown-up" step. **HAPPENED:** ONE click on a big shiny yellow button and the game was GONE, replaced in the same tab by a real Stripe credit-card checkout page ("Tews Inc", live). No confirmation, no "this costs real money, are you sure?", nothing between a kid's tap and a payment form. I backed out immediately (the game did offer "Continue game" on return, which was nice). **FEELING:** for Leo: "whoa, a credit card page, I'm gonna be in trouble." For his mom: alarm. **SEVERITY:** would-quit (kid closes the tab scared) and the top thing to fix for a kids-adjacent game.

8. **WHERE:** Game 3 win, "Five in a row. Dungeon is now unlocked!" **EXPECTED:** fireworks. I just unlocked a world with a DRAGON in it. **HAPPENED:** one small gray line of text under "You win!". Same size as everything else. The biggest reward moment in the game is whispered. **SEVERITY:** annoyance (missed celebration).

9. **WHERE:** Game 2, whole match. **HAPPENED (small oddity):** every time a spot got emptied by a capture, the computer often immediately put a new alien right back in the exact same hole. Happened at least three times. It works rules-wise, but it reads as robotic. **SEVERITY:** cosmetic.

## Moments that worked

- **Time-to-fun is great.** Robot and alien are on screen the moment the page loads (bottom bar: "Robot VS Alien"), one dropdown panel, one yellow button, playing within 30 seconds with zero reading.
- **The capture shelf is the best thing in the game.** Captured aliens stack up in little boxes at the top like trophies (1/5, 2/5...), gold-outlined, and the alien keeps my robots in HIS box, which made me furious in the good way. Winning with "You captured five pairs." and a shelf of ten aliens felt incredible. Do not touch this feature.
- **Both ways to win got understood without reading anything:** the win banner says "Five in a row." or "You captured five pairs." in words a 4th grader reads fine, and the counters at 0/5 make you ask the right question ("out of five WHAT?" - answered the first time you take a pair).
- **The unlock ladder is fair and fast:** "Finish 1 more game to unlock the Dungeon world", the profile bar says "2 of 3 games played. Finishing counts, win or lose." - even LOSING counts, so a kid is never stuck. Three games in, I'm playing as a Dragon vs a Knight in a torchlit castle. Locked stuff made me want to keep playing, not feel cheated.
- **Locked cards tell you exactly what to do** ("Play 1 more game") and the next teaser names the figures ("Dragon, Wizard, Knight, Goblin") - that list alone sells the grind.
- **The pieces menu is a toy in itself:** swap to Astronaut or UFO mid-game, instantly shows on the board, and the turn text becomes "UFO's move" which made me laugh. TYPE Flat/Chip/Half/Tall and the 3D finish visibly change the figures.
- **Backgrounds apply instantly and are genuinely cool** - picked "Asteroid Field" and rocks floated across the whole page. Dungeon world's brick-and-torches wall is a real payoff.
- **Pass & Play is understandable:** the header literally says "Robot's move" / "Alien's move", so you know when to shove the laptop at your friend.
- **Easy is beatable by a kid who reads nothing** (won in 5 moves) - arguably a touch TOO beatable, see friction #3.
- **Profile page reads like a trophy room:** big 2 PLAYED / 2 WON / 0 LOST tiles, worlds grid, figures row filling in 8 of 40.

## Words I did not understand

- "Beta" (top badge)
- "AI level" (knows "AI" vaguely; "level" here reads as game-level, not difficulty)
- "FINISH" (Classic / 3D) - thinks it means finishing the game
- "NEUTRAL" (background column header)
- "Sepia", "Parchment", "Crimson", "Nebula" (background names; "Slate" too)
- "Daimyo", "Geisha" (Japan figures)
- "LOCAL PROFILE" (badge under his name)
- "Restore" ("Already bought it? Restore")
- "Flank" (in the one-line rules: "Flank two enemy pieces to capture them" - this is THE key verb of the game and a 10-year-old does not know it; "trap" or "sandwich" is his word)
- "WIN %" (percent is 5th-grade math; he half-gets it)
- ("Skip the grind" he actually DOES understand - Roblox kid.)

## Did I understand why I won or lost?

- **Game 1, Easy, WON:** Yes. Banner said "Five in a row." and my five robots were visibly lined up. Clear.
- **Game 2, Medium, WON:** Yes for the win ("You captured five pairs." + the full shelf of 5 alien pairs, unambiguous). Only PARTLY for the middle of the game: when the AI captured MY pairs I understood it from the counter and the alien's trophy box, NOT from the board, because my captured robots stayed visibly standing long after they were gone (friction #4). The suicide-move rule (placing between two enemies is safe) worked correctly when I tested it, but nothing ever explains it.
- **Game 3, Easy, WON:** Yes. "Five in a row. Dungeon is now unlocked!" - win reason and reward in one line.

## The one change

Make captures LOOK like the event they are, in the moment they happen: the flanked pair should instantly and visibly leave the board (beam-up, poof, anything) the second the counter ticks, because right now the best mechanic in the game is silent, and captured pieces can keep standing on the board long after they are gone, which actively misleads the player. (Non-fun runner-up the developer must still hear: put one confirm step between the yellow "Unlock all worlds $2.59" button and the live Stripe credit-card page - a kid reached a real payment form in a single click.)
