Yes, she plays again tomorrow, and she texts the friend back: games are 3-5 minutes, the "one more game unlocks Dungeon" hook landed exactly when her curiosity was fading, and Medium genuinely fought back - but she almost never knew which piece the computer had just played, and that made the losses feel foggier than they were.

## Moments that worked

- **The one-sentence rules line** ("Five in a row wins. Capturing five pairs also wins. Flank two enemy pieces to capture them.") is exactly my reading budget, and it's visible on the board screen, not buried in a help page.
- **The capture shelf.** Captured pieces physically move to your opponent's tray at the top. Score isn't a number, it's little prisoners. The first-capture toast pointing at the shelf is the single best teaching moment in the game.
- **Winning lines are circled** on game over, and the banner says WHY ("Five in a row." / "The AI made five in a row."). I never wondered what ended a game.
- **The unlock curve is perfectly tuned for a first session:** "Finish 3 more games to unlock the Dungeon world" → played 3 → "Dungeon is now unlocked!" ON the win banner → new world is genuinely, visibly different (torchlit brick, goblin vs knight). That loop alone got 15 extra minutes out of me.
- **Profile copy:** "2 of 3 games played. Finishing counts, win or lose." is lovely - it tells an about-to-lose player their game still counts. "LOCAL PROFILE" badge quietly answers "did I need an account?" (no).
- **Live-preview personalization.** Change a piece or color and the board, header, and bottom bar update instantly. The GOLD color swatch with "win 9 more Space games" is a good tease.
- **"Continue game"** appeared after I reloaded the page mid-match. A browser game that survives a refresh feels trustworthy.
- **Easy is beatable by a total beginner, Medium is not.** That's the right ladder shape - my friend's crying nephew checks out.
- **Locked worlds in the theme list show their price in games** ("🔒 Ocean (6 more games)"), which reads as an invitation, not a wall.

## Words I did not understand

- "pairs" (in "0/5 pairs") - meaningless until my first capture; then perfect.
- "Chip" / "Half" (piece TYPE options) - poker chip? half of what? The previews mostly explain it.
- "Finish: Classic / 3D" - "Finish" as a noun took a beat.
- "Geisha", "Daimyo" (locked figure names) - no idea, but the little pictures carry it.
- "Neutral" (background column header) - plain-colors, I guessed right.
- "Deck Plate", "Holo Panel", "Kaya"-style board names - flavor words, the thumbnails do the work.
- Notably: ZERO board-game jargon anywhere. No "Pente", "Gomoku", "tria", "open four". As the person who's never heard of any of those: thank you.

## Did I understand why I won or lost?

- **Game 1 (Easy, won):** Yes. "You win! Five in a row." with my five circled. Though I won without ever seeing the capture rule in action.
- **Game 2 (Medium, lost):** Mostly. "AI wins. The AI made five in a row." with the diagonal circled - I could see the line. What the game never showed me is that its four had been open at both ends, so my block was already too late; I understood the WHAT but not the WHERE-I-went-wrong.
- **Game 3 (Easy, won):** Yes - five circled, plus "Dungeon is now unlocked!" stacked on top, which is the best win screen of the three.

## The one change

**Mark the opponent's most recent move** - a glow, a bounce, a ring, anything that survives two seconds. Every downstream confusion I had (clicking occupied spots, threes "appearing from nowhere", the both-ends-open four ambush, even understanding captures) starts with not noticing what the AI just did. It's the cheapest fix that makes losses feel fair and would have kept me from my one almost-quit moment.
