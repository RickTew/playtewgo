TEWGO chip test

One chip, every candidate treatment, on the same ground at the same size. Three goes at tuning the chip in isolation were all caught, so this decides it once for all 38 pieces in both engines instead of per piece. The starting point is your own diagnosis: "the chip lost 3D likely because of the bottom edge lost its line." Killing the double circle removed the rim's stroke entirely, and the OUTER bottom arc went with it – that arc is the silhouette of a disc with a side, the thing that makes a backgammon chip a chip. The INNER edge (face against rim) is the one that was doubling up, and it is the one still switched on today. Everything here is drawn by the same drawChipDisc the board uses, so nothing on this page is a look the game cannot produce. Name the yellow code of the one you want.

1 · The edges

The one question underneath all three failed attempts. Break-out is held at today's 1.15 and the shading is untouched, so the ONLY thing changing across these four is which edges are drawn. D2 is your diagnosis rendered exactly: outer bottom arc back, inner edge gone. D3 is the double circle, here on purpose so it can be ruled out on sight rather than from memory.

2 · How much head breaks out

Way out (a): pull the break-out target back from 1.15 so more disc shows. This is the structural answer to the real problem underneath the shading – on the robot, astronaut and UFO the head covers nearly the whole face, so there is barely any disc left to read as 3D, which is why the alien's chip looks fine and theirs does not. Tuning the shading of a surface that is mostly hidden cannot work. All four carry D2's edges so only the head size changes.

3 · How deep the crescent

Way out (b): keep the outer edge and deepen the crescent, so the sliver of rim showing below the face reads harder. Pushing the rim further down is what widens it. Past about 0.30 the disc starts to look like two stacked discs rather than one with a side, which is the failure mode at this end.

4 · The three ways out, side by side

Each shows Chip and Flat together, because way out (c) changes both: it gives up on depth in the Chip, lets it be a head on a token, and moves the real 3D into Flat so a player still has a disc with a side to pick. Judge these as a PAIR – the two variants have to stay tellable apart at board size or there is no reason to offer both.
